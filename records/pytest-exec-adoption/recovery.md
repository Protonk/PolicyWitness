## Operational recovery

The actual candidate registry is `tests/out/pytest-owners/`. Its completed F
operations are closed; no unresolved actual candidate checkout obligation is
hidden by a passing family. To inspect a future run when ordinary execution
refuses admission, from that checkout use:

```sh
tests/run.sh --recover-ownership RUN_ID
```

This is read-only. The same command with `--apply` is a recovery-only operation;
it cannot launch tests or force admission. Closure requires either an authenticated
live owner with matching peer/token/nonce/actual lock descriptor and observed exit,
or durable matching settlement with launch authority revoked and group absence.
A reservation without any launch authorization can be revoked under a recovery
claim. An authorized-but-unsettled record remains unresolved even if its PID field
is absent. A direct command return does not establish descendant absence.

Possible live/future command obligations block the whole checkout, including later
selected cases in an already admitted run. Matching durable quiescence with
remaining isolated cleanup protects only its output. Until cleanup closes,
retention/release cannot dispose of that output. A recorded PID, elapsed time,
missing endpoint or state label alone is not permission to signal or erase it.

`recovery-index.json` enumerates every unresolved **nested fixture** registry,
its root/run/operation identity, historical process fields, artifact directory,
endpoint status, independent observation references, missing prerequisites and
an exact read-only inspection command. Some early minimal fixtures contain no
`tests/run.sh`; their entries use the neutral candidate reader explicitly rather
than invent a public launcher. Reading does not repair their missing evidence.

The intentionally uncertain state fixtures never invoked their stated command;
that is external experimental knowledge recorded by the control. It is not fed
to production recovery as a fabricated owner settlement. Actual crash-window
controls retain peer identities and kernel exit observations in
`independent-starts.json` / `independent-exits.json`. Missing authenticated owner
settlement still leaves those fixture checkouts protected. Early failed owner
controls have less durable exit evidence; preserve that uncertainty as well.
No recovery command in this index signals a historical PID. Empty leftover
socket directories remain protected where closure prerequisites are missing.

These fixture holds are scoped to their fixture roots, not the candidate's registry.
The enclosing runs are pinned independently. Do not clear the nested records to
make pruning succeed; keep their retained failure sequence recoverable. The
supported prune tool was used periodically; it found no disposable material after
all consequential observations were pinned. No fixed retention quota is imposed.
